Fund selection record

Produces a dated, printable record of what was searched, what matched, and what you chose — for the client file. Nothing typed here is saved by this tool except your own name and firm.

What these numbers are. Volatility is the annualised standard deviation of daily returns over the period shown. Maximum drawdown is the worst peak-to-trough fall. Both are calculated here from public daily closing prices, so every figure can be reproduced from the price history.

What they are not. This is realised, backward-looking risk. Risk-profiling services publish forward-looking expected volatility from a capital markets model, which is a different measure and will not agree with these figures. Past volatility is not a promise about future volatility.

Leveraged and inverse products are hidden unless you ask for them. Their volatility is measured the same way, but it describes a daily-rebalancing trading instrument rather than a holding, so the number is not comparable with the rest of the table.

The two links. The fund name opens its prices and performance page, built straight from the symbol. Factsheet searches Trustnet for that share class. It is a search rather than a direct link because Trustnet factsheet addresses use their own internal fund codes, which nothing in this data can predict. Check you have landed on the right share class — Acc and Inc are different factsheets — before filing anything.

The FCA column is matched to the Financial Services Register by name and links to the official entry. A dash means no confident match was found for that name — not that the fund is unauthorised. A status followed by ? means the name match was loose and may be the wrong fund, usually the umbrella company rather than the fund inside it. Click through before relying on either.

This tool measures funds. It does not rate them, rank them for suitability, or recommend anything. Suitability remains the adviser's judgement.
